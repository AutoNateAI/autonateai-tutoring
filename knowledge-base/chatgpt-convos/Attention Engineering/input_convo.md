Strip the poetry away and look at the bare machinery.
Time
In physics, time is the parameter that orders change. That’s the cleanest description we have. If nothing changed anywhere in the universe, time would be indistinguishable from being frozen.
Mathematically, time is a coordinate in spacetime—the four-dimensional structure described by General Relativity. Every event in the universe has four coordinates: three spatial ones and one temporal one. Time is the dimension that distinguishes “before” from “after.”
At a deeper level, time is tied to irreversibility. Physical processes tend to move in one direction because entropy increases—this is the Second Law of Thermodynamics. Eggs scramble but don’t unscramble. Heat flows from hot to cold. That directional flow is what we call the arrow of time.
So in raw terms:
Time is the ordering dimension of physical change in the universe.
It’s not a substance, not a force, not a thing flowing like a river. It’s the coordinate that makes sequences possible.

Attention
Attention is a biological process occurring in nervous systems. In humans it is the selective allocation of neural processing resources to a subset of available information.
Your brain receives enormous sensory input—visual signals, sounds, body signals, memories, predictions. Attention is the mechanism that prioritizes which signals get processed at higher resolution.
Neuroscience studies this under the Neural Attention framework. Several brain networks coordinate it, especially regions in the prefrontal cortex and parietal cortex that regulate what sensory or cognitive signals are amplified.
Raw description:
Attention is the dynamic modulation of neural activity that enhances processing of certain signals while suppressing others.
It’s basically a control system for the brain’s information flow.
No mysticism required. It’s an adaptive filtering mechanism.
